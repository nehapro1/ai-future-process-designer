Key  principles  Your  primary  goal  is  to  be  specific  and  keep  things  simple :  ●  You  should  eliminate  ambiguity,  not  describe  every  visual  detail.  ●  Add  just  enough  information  to  clearly  distinguish  an  object  or  location  from  others.  ●  Think  about  how  you  might  tell  your  friend  to  complete  an  action;  be  that  direct  and  
simple.

2.  Write  accurate  and  unambiguous  labels  
The  goal  of  these  annotations  is  to  teach  the  robot  to  understand  natural  human  instructions.  
Because
 
humans
 
describe
 
things
 
in
 
many
 
different

there

"perfect"

Instead
 
of
 
looking
 
for

single
 
correct
 
answer,
 
focus
 
on
 
sufficiency
 
and

.
 
●  Sufficiency:  Does  the  label  provide  enough  information  for  the  robot  to  succeed  
without
 
confusion?
 ●  Variety:  We  require  diversity  in  descriptions.  One  annotator  might  say  "the  red  cup,"  
and
 
another
 
might
 
say
 
"the
 
cup
 
on
 
the
 
left."
 
If
 
both
 
distinctively
 
identify
 
the
 
correct
 
object,
 
both
 
are
 
acceptable.
 
Do  your  best  to  avoid  repeating  the  same  instruction,  even  if  the  actions  in  the  video  
are
 
similar.
 
Think
 
of
 
different
 
ways
 
an
 
instruction
 
might
 
make
 
sense
 
to
 
a
 
person.
 
 
When  distinguishing  an  object  or  location,  use  your  judgment  to  describe  the  attributes  that  
feel

Ask

What’s

2.1.3  Always  describe  objects  as  real  Describe  what  you  see,  not  what  the  object  represents  or  is  used  for.   ●  Do  not  refer  to  items  as  “fake”,  “mock”,  “toy”,  or  “model”.  ○  toy  car →  small  red  car ○  fake  plant →  plastic  plant  in  a  pot  ●  Do  not  refer  to  “robot”  parts  ○  robot  hands →  hands ○  robotic  arms →  arms  
2.1.4  Use  Standard  American  English  names  for  objects  Refer  to  objects  with  their  Standard  American  English  names.  Avoid  regional  terms.  ●  trolley →  shopping  cart

2.3.2  Use  relative  referencing  
When  an  object’s  location  isn't  immediately  obvious,  anchor  it  to  stable  landmarks.  
Your  goal  is  to  isolate  the  object,  not  to  fully  describe  its  environment.  Use  as  many  reference  
points

Prioritize  static  objects  as  reference  points  Refer  to  permanent  fixtures  l ike  shelves,  walls,  or  other  stationary  objects.  ●  ✅ Good:  Pick  up  the  white  tube  that’s  on  the  shelf  beneath  the  orange  
book
. ●  ✅ Good: Grab  the  book  to  the  right  of  the  candle .
